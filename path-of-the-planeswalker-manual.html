§2 THANK YOU

Thank you for choosing PATH OF THE PLANESWALKER, a role-playing dungeon crawl set on the guild-choked plane of Ravnica — and, for those who survive long enough to earn it, the wider multiverse beyond.

To get the most enjoyment from your adventure, please take a few minutes to read through this instruction manual. It contains everything a new agent of the Guildpact needs to know: how to move, how to fight, how not to die of thirst in a stairwell, and what exactly is going on with the rumors of something ancient stirring behind the scenes.

FILE UNDER: GETTING STARTED Save often. Rest when you can. Read every scroll before you sell it. And if a follower tells you to be careful — listen.

§3 THE STORY SO FAR

The War of the Spark is over. Ravnica is still standing — barely — and the ten guilds have gone back to doing what they do best: politicking, profiteering, and pretending the last invasion didn't happen.

You are nobody in particular. A guild agent, freshly assigned to enforce the Guildpact in the depths beneath the Tenth District, where old grudges, worse monsters, and forgotten treasure all wait in equal measure.

You don't know it yet, but something is wrong across the wider multiverse — quietly, patiently wrong, in a way that traces back to a name older than any guild charter. Then your spark ignites, and you stop having a say in the matter. The spark decides where you land, one plane at a time, and there's no going back until the trail runs out. Someone else has felt this exact thing before, though — and she isn't willing to let you walk it alone.

A NOTE FROM YOUR MENTOR "I'm not doing this out of the goodness of my heart. I can't chase this thing alone anymore, and you won't survive it without knowing what I know. We do this together, or neither of us makes it."

§7 SURVIVAL, COMBAT & RIVALRY

Hunger & Thirst drain steadily as you explore. Let either hit zero and exhaustion sets in — a real penalty, not a suggestion. Eat, drink, and rest accordingly.

Combat is turn-based: you act, then everything nearby acts back. Attack rolls, armor class, and critical hits all follow familiar tabletop logic.

THE GUILD RIVALRY WHEEL All ten guilds sit on a wheel, each strong against one neighbor and weak against the other — Azorius over Orzhov, Orzhov over Boros, and so on around the circle. Fighting an enemy guild that favors you hits harder. Fighting one you're weak against costs you. Know the wheel, use the wheel.

Status effects — Poisoned, Paralyzed, and Frightened — all follow familiar tabletop-style rules. Carry an Antidote. You'll thank yourself later.

§8 FOLLOWERS & THE BAG OF HOLDING

You may recruit up to two followers at a time from contacts scattered across the floors. Give them weapons, armor, and trinkets just like your own — and hand off a potion or ration for them to use automatically when things get rough.

Followers can be downed in a fight, but not permanently lost. A potion, or any rest — short or long — brings a downed follower right back, and heals whatever injuries the rest of your party is carrying too.

A WORD ON BAGS OF HOLDING Exceptionally rare, and worth every ounce of pack space they don't take up — each grants its own private, unlimited storage tab. You may carry more than one. You should never, under any circumstances, try to store one inside another. The label is very clear on this point. Read the label.
MERGING DUPLICATE GEAR Two copies of the same weapon, armor, or trinket can be fused into one stronger piece — vendors will always do this for a fee, and the price climbs a little each time that piece has already been fused. Learning the right feat lets you fuse gear yourself, straight from your pack, no vendor required.

§9 FIELD GUIDE: HAZARDS & CURIOSITIES

Symbols below are shown exactly as they appear on your own map screen.

🔒
Locked Doors — need a key, the Lockpicking feat, or a Ghost's peculiar talent for walking through walls.
◇
Secret Doors — invisible until you're standing close. Finding one only tells you it's there; a switch hidden somewhere nearby actually opens it. The door glows amber once found and turns green once the switch is thrown.
◆
Hidden Switches — stay invisible until their linked door is found. Walk onto one to throw it, opening the door for good.
Chests — usually treasure. Occasionally something wearing the shape of treasure. Approach at your own pace.
Water Sources — free thirst refills scattered through the dungeon. Most are safe. Some are not. There's no way to tell until you've already taken a sip.
Ambush Rooms — step into the wrong room and the door may seal behind you. Someone in there needs saving. Clear the waves, break the seal, collect the reward.
The Boss Chamber — always the largest room on the floor. Always seals shut the moment you enter. Only one way out: through the boss.

§10 VENDORS & THEIR SIGNS

Each shop type marks itself on the map with its own symbol, shown exactly as it appears in-game.

Arcane Sundries — scrolls of minor magic, and an enchanter willing to bind a little more power into your gear.
General Store — rations, potions, trinkets, and an escape rope for travelers who'd rather retreat than press on.
The Wayfarer's Inn — a warm meal, a stiff drink, and a bed. Sleep here to save your progress.
Blacksmith — steel and plate, hammered fresh, for whoever can afford it.
A NOTE ON PROPRIETORS Every shop has a name and a proprietor, not just a price list — hover a vendor on the map to see who you're actually dealing with.
SANCTUARY RULES No enemy can ever cross into a shop floor, and none will approach one from a distance while you're standing there. Guild abilities and spells still can't be loosed from inside — those reach too far to count as a fair fight. But a foe already at your side will still swing back, so a weapon blocking a doorway is never a true dead end.

§11 TIPS FROM THE GUILDPACT HOTLINE

  • Merge duplicate gear whenever you can — two ordinary trinkets make one that's actually worth equipping. Learn Artificer's Eye and merging from your own Pack costs nothing at all.
  • Talk to your followers. They notice things you don't, and they're not shy about sharing rumors.
  • Reaching Level 5 changes everything. Don't rush past it without a full pack.
  • Difficulty can be changed in Settings at any time. There is no shame in Easy. There is only shame in dying to a rat you could have avoided.
  • If a vendor mentions a planeswalker who's "been seen less and less lately" — that's not idle gossip. Write it down.
